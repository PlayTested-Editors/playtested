Contributor guide

How we write at PlayTested

PlayTested is a no-nonsense game review site: honest takes from people who actually played the game. You don't need to be a professional writer. If you can explain why a game grabbed you (or didn't), you can write for us. This page covers what we value, what you can write, and how an article gets published.

What we stand for

Every article on the site follows these. If you remember one thing, make it the first one.

Play it first

Everything you write comes from your own time with the game. Never rewrite someone else's review or write a "review" from trailers. Trailer impressions are fine, as long as the title says so.

Honest, and player-first

Help the reader answer one question: is this worth my time and money? Be excited when it's earned and say plainly when something doesn't land. Liking a game enough to play it doesn't mean going easy on it.

Story first, no checklists

The main experience comes first. Do side content when it adds something, not because an achievement list says so. No grinding just to say you did it.

Patient gamer... mostly

There's no race to be first. Waiting for patches or a sale is fine, and so is jumping in on day one when something excites you. Either way, say which version you played.

Your voice, your stories

Specific moments beat generic praise. "I threw my empty gun at the last guard and it worked" tells readers more than "combat is fun". Write like you'd talk to a friend who games.

No spoilers

Never give away the ending, late twists or final bosses. Talk about how they made you feel instead: "the last few hours hit hard" is enough.

What you can write

Not every piece has to be a full review. Most of the site is actually shorter writing. Pick whichever fits how much you've played and what you want to say.

First impressions

Most common
300–500 wordsNo scoreCategory: Review

You've played a few hours, a demo, a beta or an early access build, and you have thoughts. Say how far in you are, what hooked you, what worries you, and whether it's worth keeping an eye on.

Title ends with First Impressions, or Demo / Early Access / Beta / Trailer Impressions.
e.g. 16-Bit JRPGs Still Have a Place in This Modern Gaming World | Chained Echoes First Impressions

Short review

350–600 wordsScore optionalCategory: Review

You've finished it (or played enough to judge), but it doesn't need the full treatment. A small game, a quick verdict, or one big reason to play it or skip it. Keep the Pros and Cons and the verdict; skip the long sections.

Title ends with Short Review.
e.g. Finally, a Proper Kung Fu Game! | SIFU Short Review

Full review

800–1,500 wordsScoreCategory: Review

The complete verdict on a game you've finished or played deeply. It follows the structure below: hook, premise, Pros and Cons, score, themed sections and a conclusion.

Title ends with Review.
e.g. Short but Profound Musical Masterpiece | A Highland Song Review

Articles and write-ups

Any lengthNo scoreCategory: Write-up or Tech

Anything that isn't about one game: your games of the year, top-10 lists, opinion pieces on industry news, hardware you've used, retrospectives, or tips for a game you know well.

Free-form title.
e.g. My 2025 Games of the Year · Instead of Fleeing PC, Sony Should Have Built a Day-One PlayStation Storefront

Already reviewed on the site? Write yours anyway. A game can have reviews from several authors. A second opinion, a different platform, or a look back after big patches all help readers. Read the existing review first so yours adds something new, and don't copy its points.

Have older reviews? Bring them over. Reviews you wrote on your own blog, Steam, Facebook or a forum are welcome, as long as you wrote them. Set Details → Publish date to when you first wrote it, and it appears in the right place on the site's timeline instead of looking brand new. If it was posted somewhere else first, mention where in a note to the chief editor.

Not sure which one it is? Write what you have and submit it. The chief editor can help pick the right label, and a first impression can always grow into a full review later.

What to talk about

You don't need to cover all of these. Write about what stood out to you.

  • Gameplay and level design. How it plays moment to moment, and whether the systems hold up over time.
  • Story, characters and world. Did you care? (Spoiler-free.)
  • Art, graphics and sound. Style counts as much as resolution.
  • Performance on your setup. Real-world experience, not lab benchmarks. Say what you played on: PC specs, Steam Deck, PS5, phone.
  • Time-to-fun and value. How long before it gets good, how long it lasts, and whether it's worth the price, a sale or a subscription.

The shape of a full review

Readers know where to look because every review follows the same order. Short pieces can drop sections, but keep the order.

  1. TitleA hook, then the game: Your Hook | Game Name Review. The score goes in the Score field, not the title.
  2. The hookOne or two paragraphs about your history with the game: why you picked it up, what you expected.
  3. The premiseFor someone who has never heard of it: the setting, the goal and what you actually do minute to minute. Don't skip this.
  4. Pros and ConsTwo lists under the headings ## Pros and ## Cons, 3–5 points each. An emoji at the start of each point is our house style.
  5. ScoreFor example ## 8.5/10, if you're scoring it.
  6. SectionsTwo to four themed parts (combat, story, performance…) with ### headings. Explain how things work, not just whether you liked them.
  7. ConclusionUnder ### Conclusion: is it worth playing, and who is it for?

Tip: write the game's name in bold when you mention it. Tidy formatting in the editor does this for you, and fixes Pros and Cons lists pasted from Google Docs or an AI chat.

Scoring

Scores go from 0 to 10 in 0.1 steps. Your words and your score should agree: a 7.5 reads like a fun game with real flaws, not a masterpiece. Only score what you've played enough of. First impressions never get a score.

9–10

A masterclass. Recommended to almost everyone, and a must-buy for fans of the genre.

8–8.9

Great. A strong buy for fans and an easy recommendation with minor caveats.

7–7.9

Good. Fun but flawed, or niche. Wait for a sale or a subscription if you're unsure.

6–6.9

Mixed. For specific audiences only. Consider a heavy discount or a freebie.

Below 6

Skip, unless you love the series or one very specific mechanic.

Titles

The part before | is the headline readers see first. Make it say something about the game, not just "A Review".

  • Faithful Retelling of a Gruesome Horror Game | Dead Space Remake First Impressions
  • Puzzle Your Way Through a City of Cardboard Boxes | Boxville Demo First Impressions
  • This Is What a TRUE Sandbox Detective Game Is Like | Shadows of Doubt Early Access Impressions
  • Hunt Vampires, With Style | Evil West Short Review

Use Title Case, and spell the game's name exactly as the publisher does. Don't cut "The" or "A" when it's part of the name (The Last of Us, A Highland Song).

Images

Images are optional. If you'd rather not deal with them, submit without any and the chief editor or an editor will add some.

Good

  • Your own screenshots. They're the best choice and prove you played it (Steam: F12; consoles: the Share/Capture button).
  • Official screenshots from Media → Find screenshots (Steam, or RAWG for console games). RAWG is credited automatically.
  • A short alt text on each image describing what's in it.

Not okay

  • Images from Google search or other review sites. We don't own them.
  • Screenshots that spoil the ending or a late-game boss.
  • Photos of your screen when a screenshot is possible.

Using AI

You can use Write with AI in the editor to turn your rough notes into a structured draft. That's what it's for. But the opinions are yours.

Fine

  • Turning your notes, chat logs and brain-dumps into a first draft.
  • Fixing grammar, flow and structure.
  • Reading every line afterwards and rewriting anything that doesn't sound like you.

Not okay

  • Asking AI to review a game you haven't played.
  • Keeping details you didn't experience, or "facts" you haven't checked.
  • Pasting the reply without reading it. The final article must be yours.

Fairness and disclosure

  • Say how you got the game: bought it, review copy from the publisher, or a subscription such as Game Pass. A sentence in the article is enough.
  • Say what you played on, and roughly how far you got or how many hours.
  • No paid positive coverage. If anyone offers you something in exchange for a better score, decline and tell the chief editor.
  • Be fair to other people. Criticise the game, not the developers or other players.

From draft to published

Your drafts are private. Nobody else sees them until you submit.

  1. 1
    New articleWrite in the editor. Everything saves automatically, and History keeps every version, so nothing is lost.
  2. 2
    Fill in DetailsGame name, a one- or two-sentence description, category, tags (platform and genre), and a score if it has one. Writing up something older? Set the publish date to when you wrote it (backdating).
  3. 3
    Add images, if you likeUpload your own or use Find screenshots in the Media tab. Skip this if you prefer.
  4. 4
    Submit for reviewThe chief editor and editors may edit it directly: fix or rephrase the wording, add or change images. Then the chief publishes it. If it needs more from you, it comes back with notes at the top of your article, under "Changes requested".
  5. 5
    Published under your bylineIt goes live right away, on a future date (scheduled), or with an earlier date if you backdated it. Dates are in Philippine time (UTC+8).

Before you submit

  • I played what I'm writing about
  • The title says what kind of piece it is
  • The game's name is spelled correctly
  • No spoilers for the ending or late twists
  • I said what I played on and how far I got
  • I said how I got the game
  • The score matches what I wrote (or there's no score)
  • I read the whole thing once, start to finish

Questions?

Leave a note on your article and the chief editor will see it, or email lyndon@playtested.net. Welcome aboard, and happy writing.
